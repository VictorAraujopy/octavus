# octavus

An octopus that learns to walk across the sea floor — and later to hunt a crab — through reinforcement learning. The brain and the PPO training loop are written from scratch in PyTorch; the body and the physics run on MuJoCo.

## How it works

- **The world** (`world_octavus/`): an octopus with 8 arms on a sandy sea floor, simulated in MuJoCo as sea water: Earth's gravity plus buoyancy (an octopus's tissue is ~3% denser than sea water, so only ~0.8 N of its weight rests on the floor), water forces (drag, lift and the water it drags along), and slippery skin (friction 0.3) so the suckers do the gripping. Each arm is a soft tentacle: 16 segments tapering to the tip, each able to bend both ways, twist and stretch, held by soft springs so the arm sways in the water on its own (a relaxed octopus arm is floppy: its springs take only ~7% of the muscle's force, from the measured passive stiffness). Like the octopus's muscle bands, a muscle doesn't move one joint but pulls a whole section: each arm has 4 sections with 4 muscles (bend up/down, bend sideways, twist, stretch/shorten) and the suckers of their segments, with a touch sensor on every segment. The arm stretches up to 70%, shortens 20% and pulls with 40 N, like a real octopus arm, and like real muscle it loses force the faster it shortens (Hill's force-velocity curve, with the shortening speeds measured in octopus arm muscle), so it can't whip around at full force. Its size and weight follow a real common octopus (*Octopus vulgaris*) with 60 cm arms: arms 16x longer than their base is wide and tapering to ~4 mm, a 13 cm mantle, 2.9 kg, and a web joining the arms out to a quarter of their length (only drawn). At rest the arms aren't straight: their springs curl the tips into a loose spiral. The body is generated by `world_octavus/build_octopus.py`. A siphon adds 3 more commands, 163 in total: it aims the funnel left/right and up/down (a rudder, like a real octopus steering its jet) and squirts the water in the mantle (up to 32 N, set so that it swims at ~1 m/s at most, the measured top speed of a common octopus). Like a real mantle it is a pump: a full squirt empties it in 0.44 s and it refills in 0.4 s while relaxed (timing measured in cuttlefish). And like a real octopus, whose main heart stops while it swims, it can only jet for a few metres (about 5 s) before it needs ~13 min to recover. The environment follows the Gymnasium API and places a target in a random direction every episode, 3-6 m away on the floor. Training uses a curriculum: targets start 0.5-1 m away and move out 0.5 m each time the octopus reaches half of its last 20; once they are 3-6 m away, each level lifts them 0.25 m higher (up to 6 m), so it has to swim up to them.
- **Energy** is the metabolic cost, what the food pays for: muscles pushing cost 4x their work (25% efficient), muscles braking about 1/1.2 of it, holding force costs up to 100 W per kg of muscle even when nothing moves, growing with the square of the effort (light tone is cheap, a hard clench is not), suckers hold for free (the tissue stores the tension), the jet costs its hydrodynamic power at the same efficiency, plus the resting metabolism of a common octopus of its weight (~0.7 W).
- **The brain** (`brain_octavus/brain.py`): built like a real octopus, where two thirds of the neurons are in the arms. A central network sees the whole octopus (305 numbers: where every muscle of every section is, how fast it moves, what it touches, plus the body), drives the siphon and sends each arm a short order of 8 numbers. One small arm network, shared by all 8 arms, turns what that arm feels, its order, where it sits on the body and where the target is from its own point of view into its 20 commands; since every arm uses it, each step gives it 8 examples to learn from. The critic sees the same 305 numbers and estimates how much reward is still to come. **331,247 parameters.** (Two earlier versions: a shared arm network with no central brain, which learned fast but couldn't coordinate the arms, and a single network for the whole body, kept as `Octavus_brain`.)
- **The reward** (`brain_octavus/reward.py`) is kept out of the environment on purpose: the trainer passes a `reward_fn(info)`, and the environment only reports the facts of each step. Octavus earns points for getting closer to the target (weighted so that reaching food pays back far more than the trip costs) and pays for the metabolic energy it spends (per kg of octopus), so it should find the best way to get there, crawling or swimming. It also loses a little for jerky moves and spinning. There is no rule about facing the target: a real octopus crawls in any direction and swims mantle-first. Flipping over costs nothing extra and doesn't end the episode: it has to right itself, since ending it made dying early a way out whenever living scored negative. Each of those penalties was added after the agent found a way to exploit the previous reward. The penalties for bouncing, flying and lifting its body were dropped once the world became sea water with a real energy cost: the physics now decides whether swimming is worth it.
- **The training** (`brain_octavus/train.py`): PPO — play 2048 steps, score every decision against what the critic expected, nudge the arms towards the better-than-expected moves (never more than 20% per round), repeat.

## Running

Requires [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/) and Python 3.14.

```bash
uv sync

# train (saves octavus.pt every 10 rounds and continues from it if it exists; Ctrl+C to stop)
uv run brain_octavus/train.py

# watch the trained octopus (on macOS the viewer needs mjpython).
# It reloads octavus.pt every episode, so it can stay open while training runs.
uv run mjpython world_octavus/watch_octopus.py
uv run mjpython world_octavus/watch_octopus.py --explore   # with the random tries the trainer sees

# measure how the trained octopus is doing (reached, energy, jet, saturation...);
# every run adds a row to measurements.csv to compare over time
uv run world_octavus/measure_octopus.py

# watch the untrained octopus flail around
uv run mjpython world_octavus/environment.py

# rebuild octopus.xml after changing the body in build_octopus.py
uv run python world_octavus/build_octopus.py

# open just the body in the MuJoCo viewer (the path must be absolute)
uv run python -m mujoco.viewer --mjcf="$PWD/world_octavus/octopus.xml"
```
